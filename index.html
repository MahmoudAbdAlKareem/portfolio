01 / about

From shipping features to shipping teams.

I started as a backend developer, wiring APIs together and learning what breaks in production. Somewhere between building React applications at scale and untangling architecture decisions with product teams, I noticed the work that mattered most to me wasn't only in the code — it was in how the team around the code operated.

Today I lead engineering at Foothill Technology Solutions, where I split my time between the two disciplines I care most about: giving engineers the context and room to do their best work, and staying close enough to the technical decisions to be useful when it counts. I didn't leave engineering to become a manager. I widened it.

Before this, I spent five years as a Senior Software Engineer building and maintaining React applications and component libraries, and before that, I cut my teeth on backend systems — including two years contributing to the Rainbow cloud communication platform alongside Alcatel-Lucent Enterprise, working across client apps, backend logic, and SDK integrations.

02 / philosophy

How I think about leading engineers.

A

Empowerment over control

My job is to remove ambiguity and blockers, not to make every decision. Engineers do their best work when they own the problem, not when they're handed a ticket.

B

Growing engineers

Every 1:1 is a chance to ask what someone wants to be better at in a year, then build the next few weeks of work toward it. Careers compound the same way codebases do.

C

Engineering excellence

Velocity without craft is technical debt with a delay. I hold the line on code quality, testing, and architecture even when the deadline says not to.

D

Business alignment

A team can be executing perfectly and still be wrong, if it's solving a problem the business doesn't have. I keep engineering decisions tethered to outcomes, not just output.

E

Ownership culture

I'd rather an engineer make a reversible mistake on their own than wait for permission. Ownership is built by handing over real stakes, early.
